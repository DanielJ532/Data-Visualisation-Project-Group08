General Population Road Crash Hospitalisations

Raw hospitalisation counts alone present a misleading picture of road crash risk across Australia. Major cities recorded approximately 22,000 hospitalisations annually compared to just 1,100 in remote areas. A difference that simply reflects population size rather than genuine risk. When adjusted to hospitalisation rates per 100,000 population, a different pattern emerges.

Between 2011 and 2021, remote Australians experienced hospitalisation rates between 215 and 246 per 100,000 population. This showing that it was much higher than residents of major cities, who recoded rates between 134 and 153 per 100,000 over the same period. Regional Australians also fell between these extremes, recording rates as high as 161 to 173 per 100,000. This shows that the disparity between regional and remote and major cities has shown no improvement over the decade even after policy changes. The gap between remote and major city rates in 2021 are virtually identical to 2011 suggesting that no action has been undertaken to combat these systemic issues of road infrastructure.

Solutions

reducing the over representation of the First nations in serious road crashes requires a few systematic issues. Aboriginal people face barriers to progressing through the licensing system, increasing crash risk, particularly in remote areas. Expanding culturally appropriate, community-led licensing programs and mobile testing services can reduce this risk [9].

post crash response

Longer emergency response times in remote areas increase the likelihood of serious injury and death. Investment in faster emergency communications, automated crash alerts, and better-equipped first responders can reduce crash severity and improve survival outcomes [9].

Safer transport and infrastructure

High-risk remote roads require targeted Safe System upgrades, fatigue management measures, and improved rest infrastructure. Supporting safer vehicles and alternative community transport reduces reliance on long, high-risk private travel [9].

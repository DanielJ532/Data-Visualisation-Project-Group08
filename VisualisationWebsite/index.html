Road Crash Hospitalisations by Remoteness

The studies and data gathered from the organization has shown that Australians are ranked for a rate of 4.7 deaths per 100000 population. Australia was ranked 20th out of the 34 countries [1]. Remote areas show worse severity despite lower raw counts, the demographic who represents remote communities are aboriginal and indigenous Australians. According to 2021 census 15.4 percent of the total indigenous population in Australia lives in remote or very remote areas. Comprising 150800 individuals. Indigenous Australians are overrepresented in road fatalities by approximately 3.5 times [2]. the data suggests a strong correlation between infrastructure quality, geographic remoteness, and overrepresentation of Indigenous fatalities, warranting further investigation into funding allocation. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities face road safety challenges related to the condition and standard of remote road infrastructure our visualization aims to make these disparities visible and support evidence-based discussions on where interventions and funding are most needed.

Raw hospitalisation counts alone present a misleading picture of road crash risk across Australia. Major cities recorded approximately 22,000 hospitalisations annually compared to just 1,100 in remote areas. A difference that simply reflects population size rather than genuine risk. When adjusted to hospitalisation rates per 100,000 population, a different pattern emerges.

Between 2011 and 2021, remote Australians experienced hospitalisation rates between 215 and 246 per 100,000 population. This showing that it was much higher than residents of major cities, who recoded rates between 134 and 153 per 100,000 over the same period. Regional Australians also fell between these extremes, recording rates as high as 161 to 173 per 100,000. This shows that the disparity between regional and remote and major cities has shown no improvement over the decade even after policy changes. The gap between remote and major city rates in 2021 are virtually identical to 2011 suggesting that no action has been undertaken to combat these systemic issues of road infrastructure.
